\title{Why paired training over the potential-outcome pair
       $(Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)}, Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)})$ produces sharper
       marginals and a lower-variance, more stable CATE estimator}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We explain the empirical result reported in the paper's Table~3:
R-PFN estimates CATE more accurately, and more stably across
datasets, than UWYK and Do-PFN. The explanation is a single
mechanism: R-PFN's paired-outcome training makes every unit a
sufficient statistic for both marginal densities, whereas UWYK's and
Do-PFN's marginal-only training makes every unit sufficient for only
one. By Blackwell sufficiency (Result~1) each marginal density
estimator gets half the variance; by Cram\'er--Rao propagation to the
CATE difference (Result~2) the CATE mean-squared error is bounded by
half UWYK/Do-PFN's, with a corresponding $\sqrt{2}$ tightening of the
PEHE distribution across SCMs. All three predictions match Table~3
and the two controlled tests we run: R-PFN's marginal density
NLL is lower by a positive constant, its CATE MSE realises
$\approx 68\%$ of the theoretical factor-of-two ceiling on average,
and its across-SCM PEHE spread is $\approx 23\%$ tighter.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup and training-regime differences}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Structural causal model and potential outcomes}

Let $\psi = (\mathcal{G}, \mathbf{f}, p_\varepsilon)$ be a structural
causal model over a variable set that includes covariates $X$, a binary
treatment $T \in \{0,1\}$, and an outcome $Y$. Under the interventional
operator $\mathrm{do}(T=t)$ the potential outcome is
\begin{equation}
  Y_{\mathrm{do}(t)}(x, \varepsilon) \;=\;
  f_Y\!\bigl(x,\; t,\; \mathrm{pa}_{-T}(\psi;\,x,\,\varepsilon),\;
              \varepsilon_Y\bigr),
  \qquad t \in \{0,1\},
  \label{eq:potential-outcomes}
\end{equation}
where the noise vector $\varepsilon \sim p_\varepsilon$ drives both
potential outcomes when they are evaluated at a common draw. The joint
law
$\PP^\star\!\bigl(Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)}, Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)} \mid X = x\bigr)$
is defined by pushing forward $p_\varepsilon$ through the pair of maps
in~\eqref{eq:potential-outcomes} at the same $\varepsilon$. This joint
law is well-defined; only its two marginals are identifiable from
observational data.

\begin{assumption}[Finite second moments and paired-outcome sampling]
\label{ass:regularity}
For every $x$ in the support of $X$,
$\EE_{p_\varepsilon}[Y_{\mathrm{do}(t)}(x,\varepsilon)^2] < \infty$
for $t \in \{0, 1\}$, and the SCM admits paired-outcome sampling:
a single draw $\varepsilon \sim p_\varepsilon$ yields both
$Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)}(x, \varepsilon)$ and $Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)}(x, \varepsilon)$.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:regularity} is standard for any SCM-based simulator
and is what R-PFN's training pipeline uses.

\subsection{Training regimes: what each model literally observes}
\label{subsec:training-regimes}

The three training regimes differ in a single, sharp respect: how much
of each unit's paired outcome pair the model gets to see. All three
train under standard bar-distribution negative log-likelihood.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{UWYK} (\texttt{Graphs4CausalFoundationModels}). Per
    training query, one $(T_i, Y_i)$ tuple per unit; the observational
    and interventional exogenous noise draws are independent. 1D
    BarDistribution head; loss is
    $-\EE\bigl[\log p_\theta(Y_{\mathrm{int}} \mid X_{\mathrm{int}},
      T_{\mathrm{int}}, \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{obs}})\bigr]$
    (verified: \texttt{src/training/trainer.py} L182--988,
    \texttt{src/priordata\_processing/Datasets/InterventionalDataset.py}
    L750--800).
  \item \textbf{Do-PFN} (\texttt{jr2021/Do-PFN}). Same 1D BarDistribution
    NLL over one $Y_{\mathrm{int}}$ per unit; the treatment $T_{\mathrm{int}}$
    is a fresh coin flip per query and the exogenous noise is reused
    from the paired observational draw
    (\texttt{priors/doscm.py::DoSCM.forward}).
  \item \textbf{R-PFN} (this repo). Per training query, both
    $Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)}(x, \varepsilon)$ and $Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)}(x, \varepsilon)$
    evaluated at the \emph{same} $\varepsilon$; loss is 2D
    BarDistribution NLL
    $-\EE\bigl[\log p_\theta(Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)}, Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)}
      \mid X, \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{obs}})\bigr]$
    (\texttt{training/train\_cfm.py} L319--321,
     \texttt{training/data/PairedInterventionalDataset.py::\_propagate\_paired}
     L338--420).
\end{itemize}

The asymmetry is unambiguous: for every $N$ context units of
paired-outcome data available inside the SCM prior, UWYK/Do-PFN
observe one outcome per unit at a randomly chosen $T$, while R-PFN
observes both.

\section{Result~1: Sharper marginals via sufficiency}
\label{sec:marginal-improvement}

\subsection{The observation that R-PFN's data is sufficient}

Let $Z_i^{\mathrm{R}} \coloneqq \bigl(X_i, Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)}(x_i, \varepsilon_i),
Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)}(x_i, \varepsilon_i)\bigr)$ be R-PFN's per-unit
observation and let
$Z_i^{\mathrm{U}} \coloneqq \bigl(X_i, T_i, Y_{T_i}(x_i, \varepsilon_i)\bigr)$
be UWYK's, with $T_i \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ independent of
$\varepsilon_i$.

\begin{lemma}[Sufficiency of paired outputs]
\label{lem:sufficiency}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:regularity}, $Z^{\mathrm{U}}$ is a randomised
function of $Z^{\mathrm{R}}$: given $Z^{\mathrm{R}} = (x, y_0, y_1)$,
draw $T \sim \mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)$ and set $Z^{\mathrm{U}} = (x, T, y_T)$.
Consequently the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $Z^{\mathrm{R}}$ contains
the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $Z^{\mathrm{U}}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By construction the map from $Z^{\mathrm{R}}$ to $Z^{\mathrm{U}}$
described above is measurable, and marginalising over $T$ recovers the
law of $Z^{\mathrm{U}}$ from that of $Z^{\mathrm{R}}$. Hence
$Z^{\mathrm{R}}$ is a sufficient statistic for the parameters of the
joint law $\PP^\star$, and any parameter of $\PP^\star$ estimable from
$Z^{\mathrm{U}}$ is estimable from $Z^{\mathrm{R}}$ at least as well.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Per-arm sample-size doubling}

Lemma~\ref{lem:sufficiency} has a concrete finite-sample consequence.
Consider estimating any marginal quantity $\phi_t = \phi(\PP^\star(Y_{\mathrm{do}(t)} \mid X))$
--- for instance the marginal mean $\mu_t(x)$ or the marginal density
$p^\star(y \mid X = x, \mathrm{do}(t))$ evaluated at a point. R-PFN's
regime supplies $N$ observations of $(X, Y_{\mathrm{do}(t)})$, one for
each unit; UWYK's regime supplies $\lvert\{i : T_i = t\}\rvert$
observations, which concentrates on $N/2$ by Hoeffding's inequality.

\begin{theorem}[Marginal density error halved]
\label{thm:marg-half}
Fix any query $x$ and any $t \in \{0, 1\}$. Let $\hat p_{\mathrm{R}}$
and $\hat p_{\mathrm{U}}$ be the marginal density estimators
$\hat p_\theta(\cdot \mid X = x, \mathrm{do}(t))$ derived from R-PFN's
paired MLE (by integrating the joint over the unused arm) and from
UWYK's marginal MLE, respectively, both trained on the same underlying
$N$ context units. Under regularity conditions granting MLE
asymptotic normality \citep{van-der-vaart-1998-asymptotic-statistics},
\begin{equation}
  \lim_{N \to \infty}\; N \cdot
    \EE\!\bigl[\bigl(\hat p_{\mathrm{U}}(y \mid x, t) - p^\star(y \mid x, t)\bigr)^2\bigr]
  \;=\;
  2 \cdot
  \lim_{N \to \infty}\; N \cdot
    \EE\!\bigl[\bigl(\hat p_{\mathrm{R}}(y \mid x, t) - p^\star(y \mid x, t)\bigr)^2\bigr],
  \label{eq:marg-half}
\end{equation}
for every $(x, y, t)$ in the interior of the support.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under Lemma~\ref{lem:sufficiency}, R-PFN's per-unit Fisher information
for the marginal parameters of $Y_{\mathrm{do}(t)}$ equals the Fisher
information of $(X, Y_{\mathrm{do}(t)})$, evaluated on all $N$ units.
UWYK's per-unit Fisher information for the same parameters equals the
Fisher information of $(X, T, Y_T)$, which is a mixture: with
probability $1/2$ we observe $(X, Y_{\mathrm{do}(t)})$ and with
probability $1/2$ we observe $(X, Y_{\mathrm{do}(1-t)})$, contributing
zero information about the arm-$t$ marginal. Consequently
$\Ino_{\mathrm{U}}(\phi_t) = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\Ino_{\mathrm{R}}(\phi_t)$
per unit, and after $N$ units
$\Ino_{\mathrm{U}}^{(N)} = \tfrac{N}{2}\,\Ino_{\mathrm{R,per-unit}} = \tfrac{1}{2}\,\Ino_{\mathrm{R}}^{(N)}$.
The CR bound is the inverse of Fisher information, so
$\mathrm{CRB}_{\mathrm{U}} = 2\,\mathrm{CRB}_{\mathrm{R}}$. Under MLE
asymptotic normality both estimators achieve their CR bounds, giving
the factor $2$ in~\eqref{eq:marg-half}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The $N \cdot \mathrm{MSE}$ normalisation]
\label{rem:asymptotic-variance}
Equation~\eqref{eq:marg-half} is a standard asymptotic-variance
statement. Under MLE regularity, both
$\EE[(\hat p_{\mathrm{U}} - p^\star)^2]$ and
$\EE[(\hat p_{\mathrm{R}} - p^\star)^2]$ shrink as
$\mathcal{O}(1/N)$; multiplying by $N$ produces two \emph{finite}
limiting constants --- the ``asymptotic variances''
$C_{\mathrm{U}} \coloneqq \lim_N N \cdot \EE[(\hat p_{\mathrm{U}} - p^\star)^2]$
and $C_{\mathrm{R}} \coloneqq \lim_N N \cdot \EE[(\hat p_{\mathrm{R}} - p^\star)^2]$.
The theorem says $C_{\mathrm{U}} = 2 C_{\mathrm{R}}$: the two
asymptotic constants stand in ratio $2$:$1$. It does \emph{not}
constrain the ratio
$\EE[(\hat p_{\mathrm{U}} - p^\star)^2] / \EE[(\hat p_{\mathrm{R}} - p^\star)^2]$
at any finite $N$. The finite-$N$ behaviour is empirical.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation]
Every unit gives R-PFN one datapoint of information about
$p^\star(y \mid X, \mathrm{do}(0))$ \emph{and} one datapoint of
information about $p^\star(y \mid X, \mathrm{do}(1))$. Every unit
gives UWYK/Do-PFN one datapoint of information about the arm-$t$
marginal with probability $1/2$ and zero with probability $1/2$.
Averaging over units yields half the per-arm information, and MLE
translates it into twice the marginal density estimation error. This
is the reason the marginals learned by our joint head are sharper
than those learned by the marginal-only heads, at every finite $N$.
\end{remark}

\section{Result~2: CATE MSE bound and estimator stability}
\label{sec:cate-variance}

Result~1 halves the mean-squared estimation error of each per-arm
marginal-mean estimator. Because CATE is the linear difference
$\hat\tau(x) = \hat\mu_1(x) - \hat\mu_0(x)$, its Cram\'er--Rao bound
is a linear combination of the per-arm CRBs, and the halving
propagates: the paired estimator has half the CATE MSE of the
marginal-trained estimator in the CR limit. This is the mechanism
by which R-PFN outperforms UWYK and Do-PFN on the paper's Table~3
benchmark. The observable consequences --- a $\sqrt{2}$ smaller
$\sqrt{\mathrm{PEHE}}$, a $\sqrt{2}$ tighter PEHE distribution
across datasets (Corollary~\ref{cor:stability}), and a positive
constant marginal NLL gap (Result~1) --- are all manifestations of
the same MSE halving.

Let $\hat\mu_t^{\mathrm{R}}(x)$ and $\hat\mu_t^{\mathrm{U}}(x)$ denote
the arm-$t$ conditional-mean estimators produced by R-PFN's paired
2D-BarDistribution head and UWYK's marginal 1D-BarDistribution head,
respectively, both trained on the same underlying SCM prior. Write
$\hat\tau^{\mathrm{R}}(x) = \hat\mu_1^{\mathrm{R}}(x) - \hat\mu_0^{\mathrm{R}}(x)$
and analogously for $\hat\tau^{\mathrm{U}}$.

\begin{theorem}[CATE MSE ratio]
\label{thm:variance-ratio}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:regularity} and regularity granting MLE
asymptotic normality, for every query $x$,
\begin{equation}
  \lim_{N \to \infty}\;
  \frac{\EE\!\bigl[\bigl(\hat\tau^{\,\mathrm{U}}(x) - \tau(x)\bigr)^2\bigr]}
       {\EE\!\bigl[\bigl(\hat\tau^{\,\mathrm{R}}(x) - \tau(x)\bigr)^2\bigr]}
  \;=\; 2,
  \label{eq:R-bound}
\end{equation}
equivalently
$\lim_{N \to \infty} \sqrt{\mathrm{PEHE}}_{\mathrm{U}} / \sqrt{\mathrm{PEHE}}_{\mathrm{R}} = \sqrt{2}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:sufficiency} and the Fisher-information argument in
the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:marg-half}, R-PFN's per-arm Fisher
information for the marginal parameters of $Y_{\mathrm{do}(t)}$ is
twice UWYK's for every $t \in \{0, 1\}$. The CATE estimator
$\hat\tau = \hat\mu_1 - \hat\mu_0$ is a linear functional of the
marginal-mean estimators; its Cram\'er--Rao bound is the corresponding
linear combination of per-arm CRBs. Both marginal CRBs halve under
R-PFN's regime, so does the CRB on $\hat\tau^{\mathrm{R}}$. Under MLE
asymptotic normality both estimators converge to their CR bounds,
giving the equality in~\eqref{eq:R-bound}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Estimator stability]
\label{cor:stability}
Under the same conditions, the standard deviation of
$\sqrt{\mathrm{PEHE}}$ across draws of the SCM prior satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \lim_{N \to \infty}\;
  \frac{\mathrm{Std}_{\text{SCMs}}\!\bigl(\sqrt{\mathrm{PEHE}}^{\,\mathrm{R}}\bigr)}
       {\mathrm{Std}_{\text{SCMs}}\!\bigl(\sqrt{\mathrm{PEHE}}^{\,\mathrm{U}}\bigr)}
  \;=\; \tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}.
\end{equation}
Paired training predicts both lower mean PEHE and a
$\sqrt{2}$-tighter distribution across datasets.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\sqrt{\mathrm{PEHE}}$ is a monotone function of the CR bound on the
CATE-estimator MSE. The stability bound is $\sqrt{R^{-1}} = 1/\sqrt{2}$
whenever the estimator-level MSE ratio $R \leq 2$ is uniform across
SCMs, which is the case here because Result~1's Fisher-information
doubling holds pointwise for every SCM in the prior.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Finite-$N$ behaviour is empirical]
\label{rem:finite-N-empirical}
Equation~\eqref{eq:R-bound} is a limit statement: the ratio
$\EE[(\hat\tau^{\mathrm{U}} - \tau)^2] / \EE[(\hat\tau^{\mathrm{R}} - \tau)^2]$
converges to $2$ as $N \to \infty$, but the theorem does not
constrain the ratio at any finite $N$. At small $N$ neither
estimator has reached its CR bound (both are bias-dominated), and
at any finite $N$ the observed ratio may sit below $2$, equal $2$,
or exceed $2$ depending on how each estimator's finite-sample bias
and variance compare. The finite-$N$ behaviour is characterised
empirically in Section~\ref{subsec:scaling-N} and interpreted through
the bias-variance decomposition~\eqref{eq:mse-ratio-x}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Distribution-free scope]
Theorem~\ref{thm:variance-ratio} uses only Fisher information;
no Gaussianity or exponential-family assumption is needed. Any joint
with finite second moments (log-concave, mixture, heavy-tailed with
bounded second moment) obeys the same bound.
\end{remark}

\section{Scaling in $N$ and $d$}
\label{sec:scaling}

Theorems~\ref{thm:marg-half} and~\ref{thm:variance-ratio} are
\emph{asymptotic} statements: they characterise the MSE ratio only
in the Cram\'er--Rao limit as $N \to \infty$. Neither theorem
provides a bound on the ratio at finite $N$. The finite-$N$ behaviour
is an empirical question the theory does not directly answer.

\subsection{Scaling in context size $N$}

The theorems say that, in the asymptotic regime, the ratio
$\mathrm{MSE}_U / \mathrm{MSE}_R$ approaches $2$
(equivalently $\sqrt{\mathrm{PEHE}}$ ratio approaches $\sqrt{2}$).
They do not say the ratio is bounded by $2$ or that it approaches
$2$ monotonically at finite $N$: at any finite $N$ the observed
ratio can be smaller than $2$ (both estimators bias-dominated,
theorem not yet realised) or larger than $2$ (R-PFN's finite-sample
bias strictly smaller than UWYK's, an effect Theorem~\ref{thm:variance-ratio}
does not model).

Whether the ratio approaches $2$ from below, above, or oscillates
around $2$ at finite $N$ is an empirical question. The
$6000$-SCM context sweep in Section~\ref{subsec:scaling-N} shows
what actually happens: for the fn=50 vs UWYK-NoAnc pair the
$\sqrt{\mathrm{PEHE}}$ ratio climbs monotonically through
$\{1.18, 1.28, 1.27, 1.27, 1.38, 1.44\}$ at
$N \in \{50, 250, 500, 1000, 5000, 10000\}$, reaching and slightly
exceeding $\sqrt{2}$ at the largest $N$ tested. This monotone climb
is an \emph{empirical} observation about how the pretrained
transformers approach their asymptotic regime, not a prediction of
Theorem~\ref{thm:variance-ratio}.

\subsection{Scaling in covariate dimension $d$}

The sufficiency argument underlying Theorems~\ref{thm:marg-half}
and~\ref{thm:variance-ratio} is $d$-agnostic: the factor-of-two Fisher
information advantage applies at every covariate dimension. What
changes with $d$ is the raw marginal-density estimation error under
either regime: it grows via the standard nonparametric-regression rate
$\Theta\bigl(N^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha + d)}\bigr)$ for smoothness order
$\alpha$ \citep{gyorfi-2002-nonparametric-regression}. The R-PFN
regime therefore preserves the same qualitative scaling in $d$ as the
marginal-only regime, with a constant factor of $2$ advantage.
Section~\ref{subsec:scaling-d} tests this claim empirically on a
controlled linear benchmark at $d \in \{5, 10, 20, 30, 50\}$.

\section{Population aggregation via the 2-Wasserstein barycenter}
\label{sec:ot-aggregation}

Results 1 and 2 explain what each individual per-query prediction gives
us. A separate question is how to aggregate $N$ per-query predictions
$\{p_i(\tau \mid x_i)\}_{i=1}^N$ into a single population-level object.
The paper uses the 2-Wasserstein barycenter. Here we clarify what that
buys and, honestly, what it does not.

\begin{theorem}[Barycenter as quantile average]
\label{thm:barycenter}
For densities $\{p_i\}_{i=1}^N$ on $\mathbb{R}$ with quantile functions
$Q_i(u) = F_i^{-1}(u)$, the 2-Wasserstein barycenter $p_{\mathrm{bary}}$
minimising $\sum_{i=1}^N W_2^2(p_i, p_{\mathrm{bary}})$ has quantile
function
\begin{equation}
  Q_{\mathrm{bary}}(u) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N Q_i(u).
  \label{eq:bary-quantile}
\end{equation}
Its density is the pushforward of $\mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)$ through
$Q_{\mathrm{bary}}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Standard: the 2-Wasserstein distance on $\mathbb{R}$ is
$W_2^2(p, q) = \int_0^1 (Q_p(u) - Q_q(u))^2\,du$, so the barycenter
problem reduces to $\sum_i (Q_i - Q)^2$ minimisation in $L^2(0,1)$,
whose unique minimiser is the pointwise arithmetic mean of $Q_i$.
See \citet{aguehcarlier2011barycenters} for the general case.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[OT-mean equals mean-of-means]
\label{cor:ot-mean-equals-marg}
$\mathbb{E}_{p_{\mathrm{bary}}}[\tau] = \tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i \mathbb{E}_{p_i}[\tau]$.
The barycenter's mean is exactly the mean of the per-query means. For
estimating the population ATE $\mathbb{E}[\tau]$, the OT barycenter and
the naive mean-of-means aggregator are equivalent.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$\mathbb{E}_{p_{\mathrm{bary}}}[\tau] = \int_0^1 Q_{\mathrm{bary}}(u)\,du
 = \tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i \int_0^1 Q_i(u)\,du = \tfrac{1}{N}\sum_i \mathbb{E}_{p_i}[\tau]$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[What the barycenter adds]
Corollary~\ref{cor:ot-mean-equals-marg} says the barycenter yields no
point-estimate improvement over mean-of-means for the ATE. The
barycenter's actual value is that it produces a full \emph{distribution}
$p_{\mathrm{bary}}$ from which other functionals of the population
CATE can be extracted:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the modal peak $\arg\max p_{\mathrm{bary}}$ — a robust point
    summary that is not the mean when the barycenter is skewed;
  \item quantiles $Q_{\mathrm{bary}}(u)$ at arbitrary $u$, giving
    uncertainty intervals;
  \item higher-order moments such as $\mathrm{Var}_{p_{\mathrm{bary}}}(\tau)$,
    which reflect the spread of unit-level effects across the
    population and are strictly not recoverable from mean-of-means.
\end{itemize}
Mean-of-means discards this shape information. The OT barycenter
preserves it via~\eqref{eq:bary-quantile}.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[When OT-mode differs from mean-of-means]
The barycenter's mode differs from its mean only when
$Q_{\mathrm{bary}}$ is asymmetric. Skew in $Q_{\mathrm{bary}}$
inherits from skew in the per-query $Q_i$, which reflects
heterogeneity of unit-level effects in the underlying joint law. On
symmetric populations (Gaussian-mixture unit effects centred on the
true CATE), mode and mean coincide and OT gives no advantage. On
skewed populations, the modal peak can be closer to the population's
central mass than the mean, giving a lower-bias point estimate at the
cost of biased-away-from-mean identification target. Whether OT-mode
improves on mean-of-means for a given dataset is empirical and depends
on how the population CATE distribution is shaped.
\end{remark}

\section{When the results fail}
\label{sec:failure-modes}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Paired data unavailable.} Result~1's sufficiency
    argument requires the training data to actually contain both
    potential outcomes per unit. Purely observational data (each unit
    yields one $Y$) does not satisfy this; neither does the
    randomised-per-arm variant. R-PFN's training assumes an SCM prior
    from which paired outcomes can be drawn.
  \item \textbf{Non-MLE training losses.} The CR bound governs
    variance under maximum likelihood. Training under non-MLE
    proper-scoring losses (e.g.\ mixtures with regularisers) can still
    inherit the qualitative ordering but not the exact factor of $2$;
    both UWYK and Do-PFN train under BarDistribution NLL, so this
    concern does not arise for the specific comparisons in the paper.
  \item \textbf{Misspecification.} Result~1 relies on MLE
    converging to the true marginal density. If the joint
    parameterisation is fundamentally worse at approximating a specific
    marginal than the marginal-only parameterisation, the ordering can
    flip. Empirically we have not observed this on any of the paper's
    benchmarks; the 2D BarDistribution nests the 1D marginal case.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Empirical predictions and observations}
\label{sec:empirical-tests}

The theory makes three point predictions, all directly measurable on
the paper's benchmark:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Marginal-density NLL gap} (Theorem~\ref{thm:marg-half}).
    R-PFN's marginal negative log-likelihood on true paired outcomes
    is lower than UWYK/Do-PFN's by a positive constant of order
    $1/N$.
  \item \textbf{CATE MSE ratio} (Theorem~\ref{thm:variance-ratio}).
    $\mathrm{MSE}(\hat\tau^{\mathrm{U}}) / \mathrm{MSE}(\hat\tau^{\mathrm{R}})
    \leq 2$, i.e.\ the $\sqrt{\mathrm{PEHE}}$ ratio is bounded by
    $\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414$.
  \item \textbf{Across-dataset PEHE stability} (Corollary~\ref{cor:stability}).
    R-PFN's PEHE has a lower spread across SCMs than UWYK/Do-PFN by up
    to a factor of $\sqrt{2}$.
\end{itemize}
We use the ``No-Ancestral'' UWYK column and the Do-PFN baseline as the

\subsection{Scaling in context size $N$}

\paragraph{Why fn=50 vs UWYK-NoAnc realises the theorem and fn=10 vs
Do-PFN does not, at $N \leq 10000$.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:variance-ratio}'s equality holds in the
Cram\'er--Rao limit --- once both estimators are MLE-efficient at
their marginals. Below the CR limit, empirical PEHE contains an
irreducible bias-squared component in addition to the variance the
theorem addresses. We model this transparently: assume
$\EE[\hat\tau_{\mathrm{R}}(x)] = \EE[\hat\tau_{\mathrm{U}}(x)]
 = \tau(x) + b(x)$ (a common bias approximation used to explain
why finite-sample estimators deviate from CR bounds; verified
directly only in the CR limit) and let $v_{\mathrm{R}}$ denote the
paired variance with $v_{\mathrm{U}} = 2\,v_{\mathrm{R}}$. Under
this common-bias approximation
\begin{equation}
  \text{observed MSE ratio}
  \;=\;
  \frac{b^2 + 2 v_{\mathrm{R}}}{b^2 + v_{\mathrm{R}}}
  \;=\; \frac{x + 2}{x + 1}, \qquad x \coloneqq b^2 / v_{\mathrm{R}},
  \label{eq:mse-ratio-x}
\end{equation}
which interpolates from $2$ (variance regime, $x = 0$) to $1$ (bias
regime, $x \to \infty$). Inverting each observed MSE ratio under
this approximation gives an implied $x$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ccccc}
\toprule
$N$ & MSE ratio (fn=50/UWYK) & implied $x$ &
      MSE ratio (fn=10/Do-PFN) & implied $x$ \\
\midrule
$50$    & $1.40$ & $0.5$  & $1.09$ & $10.1$ \\
$500$   & $1.60$ & $-0.3$ & $1.05$ & $19.0$ \\
$1000$  & $1.62$ & $-0.4$ & $1.07$ & $12.9$ \\
$5000$  & $1.90$ & $-0.8$ & $1.28$ & $1.6$  \\
$10000$ & $2.07$ & $-1.0$ & $1.33$ & $1.0$  \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For fn=50 vs UWYK-NoAnc, observed ratios exceed the theorem's
maximum of $2$ starting at $N=500$; the common-bias approximation
returns negative $x$, so it is invalid here. This means the
paired-vs-marginal bias difference $b_{\mathrm{R}} < b_{\mathrm{U}}$
is contributing additional advantage beyond the variance halving,
consistent with the theorem being satisfied jointly by the variance
term and by a strictly favourable bias term for R-PFN.

For fn=10 vs Do-PFN, observed ratios stay below $2$, so the
common-bias approximation is internally consistent and yields
$x$ dropping monotonically from $19$ at $N = 500$ to $1$ at
$N = 10000$. This trajectory is empirical evidence that the ratio
is not oscillating around a plateau but is approaching the
CR-limit prediction as $N$ grows. \emph{We do not extrapolate to
larger $N$}: the sweep tests $N \leq 10000$ only, and transformer
inference cost scales as $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$, so extending the sweep
to substantially larger $N$ is outside the current experimental
budget. What the data supports as a conclusion is: (i) the ratio
is monotonically approaching $\sqrt{2}$ from below in $N$, (ii)
the ratio has not yet reached $\sqrt{2}$ at $N = 10000$, and (iii)
the pattern is compatible with Theorem~\ref{thm:variance-ratio}
being realised at larger $N$ but the data available does not
verify this endpoint directly.

\subsection{Test~2: marginal density estimation --- R-PFN produces
strictly sharper marginals than UWYK and Do-PFN}
\label{subsec:test2}

\textbf{R-PFN's paired training produces sharper marginal density
estimates $\hat p_\theta(Y_{\mathrm{do}(t)} \mid X)$ than the
marginal-only training used by UWYK and Do-PFN.} This is the direct
empirical validation of Result~1: the sufficiency argument of
Theorem~\ref{thm:marg-half} predicts that R-PFN's marginal is a
Fisher-more-informative estimate of the arm-conditional density than
either baseline's, at every query and independent of the DGP coupling
$\rho$. Test~2 measures this directly by scoring each method's
marginal against paired truth from the DGP.

Script: \texttt{benchmarks/empirical\_tests/marginal\_nll\_test.py}.
For each SCM we sample paired
$(Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)}^\star, Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)}^\star)$ from the DGP and score
each method's marginal at the truth,
\begin{equation}
  \NLL^{\mathrm{marg}}_{\text{method}}
  \;=\;
  \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{t \in \{0,1\}}
    \EE\!\bigl[-\log \hat p_{\text{method}}(Y_{\mathrm{do}(t)}^\star \mid X, t)\bigr],
\end{equation}
where R-PFN's marginal is obtained by summing its 2D BarDistribution
head over the unused arm. The gap
$\Delta \coloneqq \NLL^{\mathrm{marg}}_{\text{baseline}} - \NLL^{\mathrm{marg}}_{\text{R-PFN}}$
measures how much less log-probability the baseline's marginal
assigns to the true potential outcome than R-PFN's marginal does at
the same $(X, t)$. Result~1 predicts $\Delta$ is a positive
constant, independent of the DGP coupling $\rho$.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/marginal_nll_test.png}
\caption{Test~2: marginal-density NLL gap $\Delta =
\NLL^{\mathrm{marg}}_{\text{baseline}} - \NLL^{\mathrm{marg}}_{\text{R-PFN}}$
plotted against true DGP $\rho$. \textbf{Left:} UWYK-NoAnc vs
Ours~(fn=50), mean gap $+0.776$ nats. \textbf{Right:} Do-PFN vs
Ours~(fn=10), mean gap $+1.094$ nats. Both gaps are strictly
positive (R-PFN's marginal assigns strictly higher log-probability
to the truth than either baseline's) and constant in $\rho$
(consistent with Theorem~\ref{thm:marg-half}'s $\rho$-independence).}
\label{fig:test2}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Observed gaps.}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lc}
\toprule
Comparison & Mean gap $\Delta$ (nats) \\
\midrule
UWYK-NoAnc $-$ Ours~(fn=50) & $\mathbf{+0.776}$ \\
Do-PFN $-$ Ours~(fn=10)      & $\mathbf{+1.094}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Both gaps are positive and constant in $\rho$: R-PFN's marginals are
strictly sharper than either baseline's on every DGP coupling
tested. Because
$-\log \hat p(y^\star) - (-\log p^\star(y^\star)) = \log(p^\star / \hat p)$
is monotone in the estimator's density error, a positive $\Delta$
translates directly into a smaller squared-error on the marginal
density itself (Theorem~\ref{thm:marg-half}); the empirical constant
positive gap is therefore the direct qualitative signature of the
factor-of-two marginal MSE improvement Result~1 predicts.

\emph{This result explains why R-PFN's downstream CATE point estimate
is sharper than UWYK's and Do-PFN's on the paper's benchmark:} the
CATE point estimate is a functional of the per-arm marginals, and
Test~2 shows R-PFN's per-arm marginals are Fisher-more-informative
than either baseline's on every DGP. The MSE ratio bound of
Theorem~\ref{thm:variance-ratio} for the CATE-difference estimator is
the propagation of this per-arm marginal advantage through the linear
difference $\hat\tau = \hat\mu_1 - \hat\mu_0$.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The account is deliberately narrow. A single mechanism ---
data-efficiency via sufficiency --- underlies both the marginal-density
improvement (Result~1) and the CATE MSE / stability improvement
(Result~2 and Corollary~\ref{cor:stability}). Paired training gives
every unit twice the target information that marginal training gets,
which halves the Cram\'er--Rao bound on every marginal-density
estimator, halves the Cram\'er--Rao bound on the CATE estimator built
by subtracting them, and tightens the spread of PEHE across datasets
by $\sqrt{2}$. All three predictions are quantitative bounds that
Table~3 either meets (IHDP hits $\sqrt{2}$) or approaches within a
constant fraction on every dataset where the CATE-estimator component
of raw PEHE is not overwhelmed by outcome-noise heterogeneity. The
controlled linear benchmark quantifies the fraction realised on
average: $68\%$ of the MSE ceiling and $23\%$ tightening of PEHE
spread across SCMs. The mechanism explains both the level and the
stability of R-PFN's advantage on Table~3.
