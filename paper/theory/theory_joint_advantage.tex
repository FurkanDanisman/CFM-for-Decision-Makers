\title{Why joint training over the paired potential-outcome tuple
       $(Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)}, Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)})$ strictly dominates
       marginal-only training}
\author{}
\date{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
This note formalises the theoretical advantage of R-PFN's paired-outcome
training over marginal-only training, as used by UWYK and Do-PFN, on
both marginal density estimation and conditional-average-treatment-effect
(CATE) estimation. We isolate three distinct improvements
--- (i) a distributional bound on cross-entropy equal to the conditional
mutual information $I(Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)};\,Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)} \mid X)$,
(ii) a variance-ratio bound on the $\hat\tau$ estimator of the form
$R(\rho) = 2/(1-\rho)$, and (iii) a Fisher-information efficiency bound
on the marginal-mean estimator $\hat\mu_t$ --- and give the conditions
each requires, the regimes where each dominates, and the empirical test
that can either validate or falsify the story on Table~3 of the paper.
The three results share a single underlying quantity, the coupling
mutual information $I(Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)};\,Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)} \mid X)$,
so the overall empirical footprint is bounded by that quantity, not by
its sum.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup and training-regime differences}
\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{Structural causal model and potential outcomes}

Let $\psi = (\mathcal{G}, \mathbf{f}, p_\varepsilon)$ be a structural
causal model over a variable set that includes covariates $X$, a
binary treatment $T \in \{0,1\}$, and an outcome $Y$. Denote by
$\varepsilon$ the joint exogenous noise vector, with distribution
$p_\varepsilon$ independent of $X$. Under the interventional operator
$\mathrm{do}(T = t)$ the outcome is
\begin{equation}
  Y_{\mathrm{do}(t)}(x, \varepsilon)
  \;=\; f_Y\!\bigl(x,\; t,\; \mathrm{pa}_{-T}(\psi;\,x,\,\varepsilon),\;
                    \varepsilon_Y\bigr),
  \qquad t \in \{0,1\},
  \label{eq:potential-outcomes}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{pa}_{-T}$ denotes the non-treatment parents of $Y$ in
$\mathcal{G}$ and $\varepsilon_Y$ the noise term at $Y$. When we speak
of the joint law $\PP^\star(Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)},\,Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)} \mid X = x)$
we always mean the law induced by $x \mapsto \bigl(Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)}(x,\varepsilon),\,
Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)}(x,\varepsilon)\bigr)$ with $\varepsilon \sim p_\varepsilon$
and the \emph{same} $\varepsilon$ used in both coordinates. This joint
law is well-defined; only its two marginals are identifiable from
observational data alone.

\begin{assumption}[Finite second moments and identifiable coupling]
\label{ass:regularity}
For every $x$ in the support of $X$,
$\EE_{p_\varepsilon}[Y_{\mathrm{do}(t)}(x,\varepsilon)^2] < \infty$
for $t \in \{0, 1\}$, and the SCM $\psi$ admits paired-outcome sampling:
we can draw $\varepsilon \sim p_\varepsilon$ once and evaluate both
$Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)}(x, \varepsilon)$ and $Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)}(x, \varepsilon)$
under it.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:regularity} is standard for any SCM-based simulator
and is what R-PFN's training pipeline exploits; see
Section~\ref{subsec:training-regimes}.

\subsection{Training regimes: what each model literally sees}
\label{subsec:training-regimes}

The three training regimes differ crucially in what each training
tuple contains. All three are trained by maximum likelihood on the
respective bar-distribution head.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{UWYK} (`Graphs4CausalFoundationModels`). Each training
    query supplies $(X_{\mathrm{obs}}, T_{\mathrm{obs}}, Y_{\mathrm{obs}},
    X_{\mathrm{int}}, T_{\mathrm{int}}, Y_{\mathrm{int}})$; observational
    and interventional exogenous noise are drawn \emph{independently};
    only one outcome per unit is observed. The head is a 1D
    BarDistribution over $Y$; the loss is
    $\NLL = -\EE\bigl[\log p_\theta(Y_{\mathrm{int}} \mid X_{\mathrm{int}},
      T_{\mathrm{int}}, \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{obs}})\bigr]$
    \citep[training loop and BarDistribution loss verified in]
    []{ReuterCausalFM}.
  \item \textbf{Do-PFN} (`jr2021/Do-PFN`). Same 1D BarDistribution loss
    over $Y_{\mathrm{int}}$, but with a subtle upgrade: the interventional
    noise is \emph{reused} from a paired observational draw
    (`priors/doscm.py::DoSCM.forward`). Still only one interventional
    outcome per unit, at a coin-flipped $T_{\mathrm{int}} \in \{t_1, t_2\}$
    per query. Output is a marginal
    $p_\theta(y \mid x_{\mathrm{int}}, \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{obs}})$
    conditioned on the intervened $T$-column of $x_{\mathrm{int}}$.
  \item \textbf{R-PFN} (this repo). Each training query supplies
    \emph{both} $Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)}$ and $Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)}$ evaluated at
    the \emph{same} exogenous draw $\varepsilon$
    (`training/data/PairedInterventionalDataset.py::\_propagate\_paired`);
    the head is 2D BarDistribution with $K^2 + 9 + 4$ logits over
    $\mathbb{R}^2$; loss is
    $\NLL = -\EE\bigl[\log p_\theta(Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)}, Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)}
      \mid X, \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{obs}})\bigr]$
    (`training/train_cfm.py:319--321`,
     `losses/BarDistribution2D.py::neg\_log\_prob\_2d`).
\end{itemize}

The essential asymmetry is unambiguous: only R-PFN's training data
directly identifies the joint $\PP^\star(Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)},\,Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)}
\mid X)$; UWYK and Do-PFN can only identify its two marginals.

\subsection{Model factorisations at inference}
\label{subsec:factorisations}

Denote by $\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}$ the family of models factorising as
$p_\theta^{\mathrm{marg}}(y_0, y_1 \mid x) =
 p_\theta(y_0 \mid x)\, p_\theta(y_1 \mid x)$;
this is the largest factorised model class consistent with UWYK / Do-PFN
outputs, because each of those two models produces only a marginal.
Denote by $\Theta_{\mathrm{joint}}$ the family of joint models
$p_\theta^{\mathrm{joint}}(y_0, y_1 \mid x)$ that R-PFN realises via
its 2D BarDistribution head. Clearly
$\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}} \subsetneq \Theta_{\mathrm{joint}}$ whenever
the joint parameterisation admits non-product distributions --- which
the 2D BarDistribution head does.

\section{Main results}
\label{sec:main-results}

We now state the three theoretical results that jointly explain the
empirical improvement of R-PFN over UWYK and Do-PFN.

\subsection{Result A: distributional cross-entropy gap}
\label{subsec:result-A}

\begin{proposition}[Marginal-learning cross-entropy gap]
\label{prop:marginal-gap}
Assume the joint parameterisation is rich enough that some
$\theta^\star \in \Theta_{\mathrm{joint}}$ realises the data-generating
joint $\PP^\star(\cdot, \cdot \mid X)$ almost surely. Let
$\hat\theta_{\mathrm{marg}}$ and $\hat\theta_{\mathrm{joint}}$ denote
the MLE within $\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}$ and $\Theta_{\mathrm{joint}}$
respectively. Then the population cross-entropies satisfy
\begin{equation}
  \EE_{\PP^\star}\!\bigl[-\log p_{\hat\theta_{\mathrm{marg}}}^{\mathrm{marg}}(y_0, y_1 \mid X)\bigr]
  \;-\;
  \EE_{\PP^\star}\!\bigl[-\log p_{\hat\theta_{\mathrm{joint}}}^{\mathrm{joint}}(y_0, y_1 \mid X)\bigr]
  \;=\;
  I\!\bigl(Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)} ;\, Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)} \mid X\bigr),
  \label{eq:mi-gap}
\end{equation}
where $I(\cdot ; \cdot \mid X)$ is the conditional mutual information
under $\PP^\star$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under the realisability assumption, MLE within $\Theta_{\mathrm{joint}}$
converges (in KL) to $\PP^\star$, whose cross-entropy against itself
is the conditional joint entropy
$H(Y_0, Y_1 \mid X) = H(Y_0 \mid X) + H(Y_1 \mid Y_0, X)$. Within
$\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}$, MLE converges to the projection
\begin{equation}
  \arg\min_{\theta \in \Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}}
  \KL\!\bigl(\PP^\star \,\|\, p_\theta^{\mathrm{marg}}\bigr)
  \;=\;
  \PP^\star(y_0 \mid x)\,\PP^\star(y_1 \mid x),
\end{equation}
whose cross-entropy against $\PP^\star$ is
$H(Y_0 \mid X) + H(Y_1 \mid X)$. Subtracting gives the difference
$H(Y_1 \mid X) - H(Y_1 \mid Y_0, X) = I(Y_0 ; Y_1 \mid X)$ by
definition of conditional mutual information.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Cross-entropy vs.\ negative log-likelihood]
Population cross-entropy is the ceiling of test negative log-likelihood
in the limit of infinite context. In finite samples, both models pay
an additional $\mathcal{O}(K/N)$ excess-risk term due to estimation
error \citep{shalev-shwartz-ben-david-2014-slt}; that additional term is
bounded by model capacity $K$ and therefore favours the smaller
$\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}$ in isolation. The MI gap in
Proposition~\ref{prop:marginal-gap} is the model-class-inflicted bias
--- a constant that does not shrink with $N$. Empirically the MI gap
dominates the capacity term at moderate $N$ for the SCM priors used
here (both UWYK and Do-PFN and R-PFN are heavily over-parameterised
relative to per-query supervision).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Result B: variance-ratio bound on the CATE estimator}
\label{subsec:result-B}

Result~A is a bound on \emph{distribution} learning. Result~B is a
bound on the specific downstream estimator $\hat\tau$.

Let $\hat\mu_t^{\mathrm{marg}} = N^{-1} \sum_{i:\,T_i = t} Y_i$
under the marginal-only estimator and
$\hat\mu_t^{\mathrm{joint}} = N^{-1} \sum_{i=1}^{N} Y_{\mathrm{do}(t)}(i)$
under the paired-joint estimator; both are unbiased for $\mu_t$. Write
$\rho \coloneqq \Corr(Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)},\,Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)})$ and
$\sigma_t^2 \coloneqq \Var(Y_{\mathrm{do}(t)})$.

\begin{theorem}[Variance-ratio bound, distribution-free]
\label{thm:variance-ratio}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:regularity}, the ratio of variances is
\begin{equation}
  R(\rho, \sigma_0, \sigma_1)
  \;\coloneqq\;
  \frac{\Var\bigl(\hat\tau^{\,\mathrm{marg}}\bigr)}
       {\Var\bigl(\hat\tau^{\,\mathrm{joint}}\bigr)}
  \;=\;
  \frac{2\,(\sigma_0^2 + \sigma_1^2)}
       {\sigma_0^2 + \sigma_1^2 - 2\rho\,\sigma_0\,\sigma_1}
  \;\;\underset{\sigma_0 = \sigma_1}{=}\;\;
  \frac{2}{1 - \rho}.
  \label{eq:R-rho}
\end{equation}
$R$ is strictly increasing in $\rho \in (-1, 1)$, satisfies $R(0) = 2$,
and $R(\rho) \to \infty$ as $\rho \to 1^{-}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under the marginal-only regime, each arm gets $N/2$ independent samples,
so $\Var(\hat\tau^{\,\mathrm{marg}}) = \Var(\hat\mu_1^{\mathrm{marg}}) +
\Var(\hat\mu_0^{\mathrm{marg}}) = (\sigma_0^2 + \sigma_1^2)/(N/2) =
2(\sigma_0^2 + \sigma_1^2)/N$. Under the paired-joint regime, both
arms are computed on each of the $N$ paired draws, so
$\hat\tau^{\,\mathrm{joint}} = N^{-1}\sum_i (Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)}(i) -
Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)}(i))$ has variance $(\sigma_0^2 + \sigma_1^2 - 2\rho
\sigma_0 \sigma_1)/N$. The ratio is~\eqref{eq:R-rho}. Monotonicity is
$\partial R / \partial \rho > 0$ for $\rho < 1$; divergence is the limit
$\sigma_0^2 + \sigma_1^2 - 2\rho\sigma_0\sigma_1 \to (\sigma_0-\sigma_1)^2$
which is zero at $\sigma_0 = \sigma_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Distribution-free scope]
Theorem~\ref{thm:variance-ratio} uses only first and second moments of
the paired law; no Gaussianity or exponential-family assumption is
needed. Any joint with finite second moments (log-concave, mixture,
heavy-tailed with bounded second moment, \ldots) obeys the same bound.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Result C: Fisher-efficiency bound on marginal means}
\label{subsec:result-C}

Result~B compares the $\hat\tau$ estimator built from equal-size
context samples. But the training regimes actually differ in
\emph{how many outcomes per unit} they observe. R-PFN's per-unit
supervision covers both arms; UWYK/Do-PFN's covers one. This changes
the Fisher information for the marginal-mean parameters.

\begin{proposition}[Marginal-mean Fisher-efficiency gap]
\label{prop:fisher}
Let each SCM sample produce a triple $(x, y_0, y_1)$ under R-PFN's
paired regime, whose joint density is $p^\star(y_0, y_1 \mid x)$ with
finite Fisher information $\Ino_{\mathrm{joint}}(\mu_0, \mu_1)$ w.r.t.
the marginal means. Under UWYK/Do-PFN's regime the same SCM sample
produces only one Y at a randomly chosen T; write
$\Ino_{\mathrm{marg}}(\mu_0, \mu_1)$ for the resulting per-sample
Fisher information w.r.t.~$(\mu_0, \mu_1)$. Then
\begin{equation}
  \Ino_{\mathrm{joint}} \;\succeq\; \Ino_{\mathrm{marg}}
  \quad
  \text{(Loewner ordering)}, \qquad
  \bigl[\Ino_{\mathrm{joint}}^{-1}\bigr]_{\mu_t, \mu_t}
  \;\leq\;
  \bigl[\Ino_{\mathrm{marg}}^{-1}\bigr]_{\mu_t, \mu_t}.
  \label{eq:fisher-order}
\end{equation}
Concretely, under jointly Gaussian $(Y_0, Y_1)\!\mid\!X$ with variances
$\sigma^2$ and correlation $\rho$, the diagonal entries of
$\Ino_{\mathrm{marg}}^{-1}$ are $2\sigma^2 / N$ (each arm sees $N/2$
samples) whereas the diagonal entries of $\Ino_{\mathrm{joint}}^{-1}$
are $\sigma^2 / N$. The Cram\'er--Rao lower bound therefore gives
$\Var(\hat\mu_t^{\,\mathrm{joint}}) / \Var(\hat\mu_t^{\,\mathrm{marg}}) \leq 1/2$
regardless of $\rho$; when $\rho \neq 0$ the ratio is strictly smaller,
because the joint estimator can borrow information across arms.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Loewner ordering is monotone under sub-$\sigma$-algebras; the
marginal-only observation is a coarsening of the paired one, so its
Fisher information is dominated. The Gaussian evaluation is direct: the
marginal-only per-sample Fisher for $\mu_t$ is
$\PP(T = t)/\sigma^2 = 1/(2\sigma^2)$, giving a diagonal
$\Ino_{\mathrm{marg}}^{-1} = 2\sigma^2 \cdot \mathrm{diag}(1, 1)/N$;
the joint per-sample Fisher for $(\mu_0, \mu_1)$ is $\Sigma^{-1}$ where
$\Sigma = \sigma^2 \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & \rho \\ \rho & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$.
The diagonal entries of $\Ino_{\mathrm{joint}}^{-1} = \Sigma$ are
$\sigma^2$ per sample, so with $N$ paired samples the diagonal is
$\sigma^2 \cdot \mathrm{diag}(1, 1)/N$, giving the ratio~$1/2$ stated.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Decomposing $R(\rho) = 2/(1-\rho)$]
Result~C decomposes Theorem~\ref{thm:variance-ratio}'s
$R(\rho) = 2/(1-\rho)$ into two pieces. The constant factor of $2$ is
the Fisher-efficiency gap for each $\hat\mu_t$ individually, and it
comes entirely from R-PFN observing \emph{both} potential outcomes per
unit rather than one --- a per-arm sample-size advantage. The factor of
$1/(1-\rho)$ is the covariance cancellation in
$\Var(\hat\tau) = \Var(\hat\mu_1) + \Var(\hat\mu_0) - 2\Cov(\hat\mu_0, \hat\mu_1)$:
it appears in $\hat\tau$ but not in each $\hat\mu_t$. Thus the
individual-marginal improvement is independent of $\rho$, whereas the
CATE-estimator improvement scales with $\rho$. Both are strict, but
they arise from different mechanisms and can be tested separately.
\end{remark>

\subsection{Summary}

Results A--C isolate three distinct effects of paired-joint training:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Result & Quantity bounded & Bound \\
\midrule
A & Cross-entropy gap over $y_0, y_1 \mid x$
  & $I(Y_0; Y_1 \mid X)$ \\
B & Variance of $\hat\tau$
  & $R(\rho) = 2/(1-\rho)$ \\
C & Variance of each $\hat\mu_t$
  & $\leq \tfrac{1}{2}$, tighter when $\rho \neq 0$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

They are neither independent nor additive: they share the underlying
quantity $I(Y_0; Y_1 \mid X)$, which is the joint law's departure from
independence conditional on $X$. When $I(Y_0; Y_1 \mid X) = 0$
(conditional independence), all three bounds collapse to no
improvement.

\section{Scaling in $N$, $\rho$, and $d$}
\label{sec:scaling}

We now derive the scaling predictions in the three natural axes.

\subsection{Scaling in context size $N$}

\begin{proposition}[Constant vs.\ vanishing components]
\label{prop:scaling-N}
Result~A's gap $I(Y_0; Y_1 \mid X)$ is a constant (in $N$) lower
bound on excess cross-entropy: it does not vanish as $N \to \infty$.
Results~B and C give ratios; they hold at every finite $N$ under the
respective per-sample estimators and are asymptotically stable.
Estimation-error terms for both regimes vanish as $\mathcal{O}(K/N)$,
where $K$ is model capacity, so the gaps in Results~A--C dominate at
large $N$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Result~A is a statement about the projection floor of MLE, which is a
property of the model class, not the sample. Results~B and~C are
per-sample variance ratios; they do not depend on $N$. Additional
$\mathcal{O}(K/N)$ estimation error is standard learning theory
\citep{shalev-shwartz-ben-david-2014-slt}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Small-sample amplification]
When context is very small, all three results are supplemented by a
regularisation effect: the joint MLE searches within a strict subset
$\Theta_{\mathrm{joint}} \cap \{\text{consistent-marginals}\}$, which
has lower estimation error than the unconstrained $\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}$.
So the fn=10/fn=50 advantage over UWYK/Do-PFN is predicted to be
\emph{largest at small $N$} and to persist (not vanish) as $N \to \infty$.
\end{corollary}

\subsection{Scaling in coupling $\rho$}

\begin{proposition}[Monotone scaling in $\rho$]
\label{prop:scaling-rho}
Result~A's gap $I(Y_0; Y_1 \mid X)$ is a strictly increasing function
of $\rho(X)^2$ for jointly-Gaussian conditionals, with the closed form
$I(Y_0; Y_1 \mid X) = -\tfrac{1}{2}\log(1 - \rho(X)^2)$. In general it
is a non-negative function of the joint law that vanishes iff
$Y_0 \perp\!\!\!\perp Y_1 \mid X$ (Shannon's information inequality).
Result~B strictly improves in $|\rho|$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:variance-ratio}).
Result~C does not depend on $\rho$ (Fisher-efficiency ratio is $1/2$
regardless of coupling; see Remark following
Proposition~\ref{prop:fisher}).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For jointly Gaussian pairs, the closed-form $-\tfrac{1}{2}\log(1 - \rho^2)$
is standard \citep{cover-thomas-eit}. For general laws we have only
non-negativity and the equality condition, both from Shannon's
information inequality. We do \emph{not} claim monotonicity of $I$ in
$\rho$ for non-Gaussian pairs --- $\rho$ is a summary statistic that
does not determine $I$ outside the Gaussian family. Result~B's
monotonicity is a direct calculation on
$\Var(\hat\tau) \propto \sigma_0^2 + \sigma_1^2 - 2\rho\sigma_0\sigma_1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[$\rho$ is a Gaussian summary]
The $\rho$-based bounds (Results~B and the Gaussian version of A) are
tight for jointly-Gaussian pairs. For pairs whose dependence is not
captured by linear correlation (e.g.\ deterministic $y_1 = y_0^2$ with
$\rho = 0$), the mutual information can be arbitrarily large even
with zero correlation. In such cases Result~A still applies via the
information-inequality bound, while Result~B does not: the
$\rho$-parameterised variance ratio only measures linear-covariance
cancellation. The paper's Table~3 datasets are all approximately-linear
in the outcome mechanism, so the Gaussian $\rho$-parameterisation is a
reasonable summary in practice.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Scaling in covariate dimension $d$}

\begin{proposition}[Sample-complexity advantage in $d$]
\label{prop:scaling-d}
Let $d = \dim(X)$. Under standard nonparametric regularity
\citep{gyorfi-2002-nonparametric-regression}, the minimax rate for
estimating a smooth conditional density $p(y \mid x)$ from $N$ samples
is $\Theta\bigl(N^{-2\alpha/(2\alpha + d)}\bigr)$ in $L^2$, where
$\alpha$ is the smoothness order. This rate applies to the
marginal-only learner's estimate of each $p_\theta(y_t \mid x)$.
The joint learner also incurs this rate on the marginal $p(y_0 \mid x)$,
but the coupling term $p(y_1 \mid y_0, x)$ can be sharper: given
$Y_0 = y_0$ as an additional observed conditioning variable, the SCM's
noise structure often makes $Y_1$ a low-dimensional function of $(y_0, x)$
--- in the extreme, an additive shift $y_0 + g(x)$.
Consequently the marginal-only cross-entropy incurs the curse of
dimensionality in $d$ twice (once per arm), whereas the joint learner
incurs it only on the first term.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}[Where the assumption is used]
Proposition~\ref{prop:scaling-d} does \emph{not} claim that
$I(Y_0; Y_1 \mid X)$ grows with $d$ as a distributional quantity ---
that would depend on the SCM. It claims that the \emph{estimation error}
on the two learners' cross-entropies scales differently in $d$, because
the joint learner's coupling term has lower effective dimensionality.
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:regularity} and any SCM where $Y_1$ is a
smooth function of $Y_0$ given $X$, the joint learner's excess-risk
term scales more favourably with $d$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Empirical implication]
The prediction is: R-PFN's advantage over UWYK / Do-PFN widens with the
number of covariates. On Table~3 datasets IHDP (25 covariates) and CPS
(24 covariates) should show larger gaps than ACIC (58 covariates but
lower-signal features) or datasets with only a handful of covariates.
This is testable directly; see Section~\ref{sec:empirical-tests}.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Scaling in unit-level effect variance}

\begin{proposition}[Small unit-level variance amplifies the gap]
\label{prop:scaling-unit-var}
When $\Var(Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)} - Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)} \mid X)$ is small relative
to $\Var(Y_{\mathrm{do}(t)} \mid X)$, the coupling correlation $\rho(X)$ is
close to $1$ and all three bounds achieve their extremes. In the limit
of a deterministic unit-level treatment effect, $\rho(X) = 1$ and
$I(Y_0; Y_1 \mid X) = H(Y_1 \mid X)$, so joint training strictly
identifies the counterfactual given only the observed potential outcome.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\Var(Y_1 - Y_0) = \sigma_0^2 + \sigma_1^2 - 2\rho\sigma_0\sigma_1$. When
$\sigma_0 = \sigma_1 = \sigma$ this equals $2\sigma^2(1 - \rho)$, so
low unit-level variance forces $\rho \to 1$ from below. The mutual-
information limit is a direct consequence of
$H(Y_1 \mid Y_0, X) \to 0$ when $Y_1$ is a deterministic function of
$Y_0$ given $X$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Real-world implication]
Datasets with tight unit-level treatment effects (typical in
semi-synthetic benchmarks like IHDP, where the treatment mechanism is
deterministic in the noise) should show near-maximal advantage for
paired-joint training. Datasets with large unit-level heterogeneity
should show smaller advantage. This is the concrete testable prediction
from the ``fixing the noise'' intuition.
\end{remark}

\section{When the results fail}
\label{sec:failure-modes}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Conditional independence.} If $Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)}
    \perp\!\!\!\perp Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)} \mid X$, then $I(Y_0; Y_1 \mid X) = 0$
    and all three bounds are vacuous. This regime is unusual in practice
    (it requires that the treatment mechanism and outcome noise are
    generated by independent-per-arm draws), but it is the theoretical
    breaking point. Randomised controlled trials with fresh noise per
    arm satisfy it by construction.
  \item \textbf{Model misspecification.} If neither
    $\Theta_{\mathrm{joint}}$ nor $\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}$ contains the
    true joint, Result~A's projection floor argument still applies but
    the numerical value of the gap can be larger or smaller than
    $I(Y_0; Y_1 \mid X)$. In particular, a joint parameterisation that
    is worse at fitting a specific marginal than the marginal-only
    class can, in principle, produce a joint MLE with worse marginals.
    We have not observed this empirically; the 2D BarDistribution's
    per-bin softmax structure is designed to nest the 1D marginal case.
  \item \textbf{Paired data unavailable.} All three results assume the
    training pipeline can jointly evaluate $(Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)},
    Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)})$ at the same noise draw. Purely observational
    training data (each unit yields one $Y$) does not satisfy this and
    the ordering of $\Theta_{\mathrm{joint}}$ vs $\Theta_{\mathrm{marg}}$
    becomes moot: neither can access the coupling.
  \item \textbf{Non-MLE training losses.} If either the base model or
    ours were trained under a proper scoring rule other than log-loss
    (e.g., Brier score, focal loss, or a mixture with regularisers),
    the ``excess NLL = $I$'' identity in Proposition~\ref{prop:marginal-gap}
    breaks. Both UWYK and Do-PFN train under BarDistribution NLL, so
    this concern does not arise for the specific comparisons in the
    paper.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Empirical tests}
\label{sec:empirical-tests}

Two concrete tests either validate the story or falsify it.

\subsection{Test 1: coupling $\rho$ vs.\ performance gap}
\label{subsec:test-rho}

For each Table-3 dataset $d$, compute
$\hat\rho_d \coloneqq \EE_x[\hat\rho(x)]$ where $\hat\rho(x)$ is the
Pearson correlation of R-PFN's joint $\{p_{j_0, j_1}(x)\}$ evaluated at
bin midpoints; equation~(6) of Section~1 of \texttt{theory.tex} gives
the exact formula. Then plot both

\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{\mathrm{marg}}^{(d)} \;\coloneqq\;
  \sqrt{\mathrm{PEHE}}_{\mathrm{UWYK}}(d) \;-\; \sqrt{\mathrm{PEHE}}_{\text{fn=50}}(d),
  \label{eq:delta-marg}
\end{equation}
and analogously $\Delta_{\mathrm{DoPFN}}^{(d)}$, against $\hat\rho_d$.
Under Results A--C, both $\Delta$'s should be strictly positive when
$\hat\rho_d > 0$ and should scale monotonically with $\hat\rho_d$.

\subsection{Test 2: unit-level effect variance vs.\ performance gap}
\label{subsec:test-unit-var}

Compute $\hat{V}^{(d)} \coloneqq \EE_x\bigl[\hat\Var\bigl(Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)}
- Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)} \mid x\bigr)\bigr]$ from R-PFN's joint (as the
population variance of $\tau$ marginalised over the query set) and plot
$\Delta_{\mathrm{marg}}^{(d)}$ against $\hat{V}^{(d)}$. Under
Proposition~\ref{prop:scaling-unit-var}, the relationship should be
strictly \emph{decreasing}: smaller unit-level variance $\Leftrightarrow$
larger $\rho$ $\Leftrightarrow$ larger advantage.

\subsection{Falsification criterion}

If either test shows the predicted relationship failing across the
Table-3 datasets, then Results A--C are insufficient to explain the
empirical improvement and additional mechanisms (capacity,
regularisation from the 9-region tail model, MALC-smoothing) must be
implicated. Both tests are computed on already-produced npz files; see
the companion script \texttt{benchmarks/plots/plot\_rho\_vs\_gap.py}.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The three results together answer the question posed in the paper's
Table~3: why does R-PFN outperform UWYK on $\sqrt{\mathrm{PEHE}}$ and
$\varepsilon_{\mathrm{ATE}}$ at matched feature budget (fn=50), and
why does the fn=10 variant outperform Do-PFN? Both improvements share
a single mechanism --- access to the joint law
$\PP^\star(Y_{\mathrm{do}(0)}, Y_{\mathrm{do}(1)} \mid X)$ that UWYK and
Do-PFN's training regime literally cannot supervise on. The
distributional (Result~A), estimator-variance (Result~B), and
Fisher-efficiency (Result~C) advantages are three faces of this single
piece of information, and together they explain both the marginal and
the joint improvement observed on Table~3.

The two testable empirical predictions --- monotone gap scaling in
$\rho$ and inverse scaling in unit-level effect variance --- are the
tightest empirical corollaries the theory produces. They should be
reported in the paper alongside Table~3 as the falsifiable
consequences of the theoretical story.
